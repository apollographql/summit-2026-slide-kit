Part 04
Breaking the rules

The recommended way, and ours.

The recommendation

Propose from a development variant

Development, testing, staging, production. It catches breaking changes early.

Cost: four stages of coordination

Northstar does

Propose against production, then check

Approval before development starts. Automated checks carry the rest.

Cost: two to three reviewers

With 186 certified engineers, the bottleneck was never their knowledge. It was the process.

Approved first, then built.

What runs when a schema changes.

Webhook

Apollo fires on every proposal. The signature is validated before anything runs.

Entry point

Lambda one

Extract hashes, diff base against proposed, identify which subgraphs moved.

Receive and initiate

Lambda two

Polls every second until the workflow completes, so nothing times out.

Monitor

Callback

Errors and warnings land back in Studio, where the developer iterates.

Continuous integration for the graph

Three things that made it hold.

→
Two Lambdas, not one. One receives and initiates, one monitors. Nothing times out.
→
Async by design. Checks run in the background and no developer waits on them.
→
Always check against production, whatever variant the developer is working on.
